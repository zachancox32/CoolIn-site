Local knowledge

What the buildings in Blackburn tend to need

Blackburn work skews commercial and industrial, and the domestic side is almost entirely pre cavity wall construction.

Solid wall terraces

No cavity, so heat crosses the wall in both directions faster than in a modern house. Both the heating and cooling loads run higher than floor area suggests, and we size on the construction rather than the square metres.

Mill buildings in mixed use

Enormous volumes, single glazing in places and exposed brick with no insulation. Conditioning the whole floor plate is rarely economic. Zoned cooling over the occupied areas is.

Manufacturing and process

Where cooling protects equipment or a process rather than people, the tolerance is tighter and a failure costs more. That usually means two systems rather than one, and remote alarms.

Darwen and the higher ground

Climbing towards the moors, colder and more exposed, with the same honest conversation about heat pump output on the coldest weeks that we have in Saddleworth.

Local questions

Asked most often in Blackburn

Is air conditioning worth it in a Lancashire mill town?

The cooling matters for perhaps eight weeks a year. The heating matters for six months, and that is where these systems earn their keep here. Warming one or two rooms you actually use, at roughly a quarter of the cost of the same heat from an electric heater, makes more financial sense in Blackburn than the summer cooling does. The cooling is the part you will be grateful for in July.

Can you cool machinery rather than people?

Yes, and it is a different specification. Process and equipment cooling runs at a constant load year round, tolerates far less variation, and fails expensively. That normally means close control units rather than comfort cooling, a second system so one failure does not stop production, and remote alerting so you know before the machine does.
