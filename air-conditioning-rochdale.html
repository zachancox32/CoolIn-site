Local knowledge

What the buildings in Rochdale tend to need

Rochdale work leans commercial and industrial more than most of our patch, and the domestic side is dominated by solid wall terraces.

Solid wall terraces

Built before cavity walls, so heat moves through the fabric in both directions faster than a modern house. The heating and the cooling loads both run higher than the floor area alone would suggest and we size accordingly.

Industrial and trade units

Dusty environments where standard filters block quickly. Heavier duty units, accessible filter positions and a service plan that reflects the conditions rather than a domestic schedule.

Town centre retail

Shop floors trading with the door open, which is a cooling load most calculations ignore. Air curtains and correctly positioned units make far more difference than simply fitting something bigger.

Littleborough and the moor edge

Exposed positions where wind driven rain hits the outdoor unit directly. Positioning and bracket choice matter more here than in a sheltered suburb.

Local questions

Asked most often in Rochdale

How often will filters need cleaning in a workshop?

Far more often than in an office. A domestic or office system is fine on an annual clean with one interim check. In a workshop, joinery unit or anywhere with airborne dust, filters can block within a couple of months, and a blocked filter costs you both efficiency and, eventually, the compressor. We set the service frequency to the environment and show you how to pull and rinse the filters yourself between visits.

Does leaving the shop door open make air conditioning pointless?

Not pointless, but it changes the design. An open door is a continuous cooling load that most sizing calculations ignore entirely. The answer is usually a combination of a correctly sized system and an air curtain over the entrance, which costs less than upsizing the cooling and works considerably better.
