Local knowledge

What the buildings in Warrington tend to need

Warrington has more new build housing than anywhere else we cover, and more warehouse floor space. Both need a different approach to an old terrace.

New build estates

Airtight and well insulated, which is superb in January and the reason they overheat in June. Solar gain through large windows has nowhere to escape. These homes often need less capacity than their floor area suggests but far more thought about which rooms are getting the sun.

Business parks

Birchwood and the office parks off the M62 run floor plates with a cold north side and a glazed south side. Zoned cassettes or VRF with proper controls stop the thermostat war that follows.

Distribution and logistics

High bay units where cooling the whole volume is never economic. Spot cooling over pick stations and offices, with destratification fans bringing the warm air back down in winter.

Stockton Heath and Lymm

Older village centres with period property and conservation constraints, sitting right next to the new build estates. Same town, completely different survey.

Local questions

Asked most often in Warrington

Why does my new build overheat when it is so well insulated?

Because insulation works in both directions. A modern home is built airtight to keep heat in, so when sunlight comes through large south or west facing windows the heat that arrives has nowhere to go. There is no draught to carry it away and the fabric is too light to absorb much. It is common for a new build to run hotter in July than a draughty Victorian house of the same size.

Can you cool a warehouse or distribution unit?

Not the whole volume, economically. Cooling a high bay shed to a comfortable temperature costs more than most operations can justify. What works is spot cooling over the areas where people actually stand, packing benches, pick stations and offices, and destratification fans to push warm air back down in winter rather than letting it sit in the roof space.
