Before we start

Builder’s work, fit-out categories and handover

The three things that most often decide whether an air conditioning package runs to programme or turns into a variation.

What builder’s work does the air conditioning need?

Every job needs some of this from the main contractor or other trades. We identify it all at tender, include what we can in our price, and list the rest so it is programmed rather than discovered.

  • Core holes through external walls for the pipework
  • Power supplies to the outdoor unit positions
  • A route for condensate to a drain, or space for a pump
  • Ceiling void depth for cassettes, usually around 300mm
  • Plinths, a flat roof position or bracket fixings for outdoor units
  • Safe access to the roof or to high level positions

None of it should arrive later as an extra. If it is not in the tender, it is our miss, not yours.

Air conditioning on Cat A and Cat B fit-outs

On a Cat A fit-out the air conditioning is usually part of the base build: open plan zones served by cassettes or VRF, ready for whichever tenant arrives.

On a Cat B fit-out the tenant’s layout arrives, so units move to suit meeting rooms and partitions, comms rooms get their own cooling, and the zones are rebalanced.

Moving a Cat A system to suit a Cat B layout is often cheaper than it looks, because the outdoor plant usually stays where it is.

What your client gets at handover

Commissioning sheets for every unit, the F-Gas record with the charge and CO2 equivalent, electrical certificates, O&M manuals and warranty registration, plus a walk through of the controls with whoever runs the building.

Where the building’s air conditioning is over the 12kW threshold, the TM44 inspection duty is flagged in the pack so it does not come as a surprise to the client later.

The pack is prepared for practical completion rather than chased afterwards.

Questions

Questions contractors ask

Can you price an air conditioning package from our drawings?

Yes. Send the drawings, the specification and the tender return date, and we come back with a price, the equipment schedule and a list of the builder’s work. Where the drawings do not show enough, such as ceiling void depths or roof access, a short site visit fills the gap.

Do you design the system or install to our consultant’s design?

Either. We install to an M&E consultant’s design and specification, or take on the design of the air conditioning ourselves, with the heat load worked out room by room and the equipment schedule to match. Tell us which the job needs in the tender.

When should the air conditioning installer start on a fit-out?

At first fix, before the ceilings close. Refrigerant pipework, condensate drains and control cables all run in the ceiling void, so bringing the installer in late means opening ceilings again. Indoor units then go in at second fix, after ceilings and decoration.

Can you work around a live building?

Yes. Occupied offices, shops and restaurants are usually done in the evenings and at weekends at standard labour rates, and larger jobs are phased floor by floor so only one area is affected at a time.

Do you take on small packages?

Yes, from a single cassette in a meeting room to VRF across several floors. Small packages are often where a specialist saves the most time, because nobody else on site wants to own them.

Can you look after the system after handover?

Yes. Servicing and planned maintenance can start at handover and run through the defects period and beyond, so the client has one number to ring. We flag TM44 inspection dates and leak check dates where the system needs them.
