What the buildings in Ashton under Lyne tend to need

Ashton and the Tameside towns around it are among the quicker addresses for us to reach, which shows up in callout response rather than in the price.

Victorian terraces

Solid wall, narrow frontage, small rear yard. The standard Tameside job, and a straightforward one once the outdoor unit position is agreed.

Canalside mill conversions

Stalybridge and the canal corridor have a good number of converted mills: high ceilings, single aspect glazing and no ceiling void, so surface trunking that follows the existing services usually beats chasing anything in.

Retail and leisure

The retail park and town centre units, trading with doors open and large glazed frontages. Air curtains and correct positioning do more than simply upsizing the system.

Rising ground towards Mossley

As you climb out of the valley the exposure increases and the winters lengthen, which is worth factoring into the heating expectation.

Local questions

Asked most often in Ashton under Lyne

How quickly can you get to Ashton for a breakdown?

Around 25 minutes, which puts it among the quickest addresses on our patch. Service plan customers get same day phone support and next working day attendance. Without a plan you go behind contract customers, which through July and August can still mean several days even from this close.

Can pipework be hidden in a mill conversion?

Sometimes, but often it should not be. Converted mills rarely have a ceiling void or stud walls to chase into, and cutting into structural brick or exposed beams is both difficult and, in a listed conversion, not permitted. The tidier answer is usually white trunking routed alongside the existing services and conduit, which in a building of that character reads as part of the fabric rather than as an addition.
