Local knowledge

What the buildings in Preston tend to need

Housing stock and building type change what a good installation looks like far more than the badge on the unit does.

New build estates

Airtight, well insulated and often overheating by April because of the glazing. These homes usually need less capacity than their floor area suggests but more attention to where the heat is coming in.

Stone and solid wall terraces

The opposite problem. Heat pours out in winter and in through the roof in summer, so both heating and cooling loads run higher than a modern equivalent of the same size.

Business parks and light industrial

Office blocks and units around the M6 corridor, where VRF systems and out of hours fitting are the norm.

Landlord portfolios

Multiple properties on one service plan, with the per unit price dropping as the number of units on the contract goes up.

Local questions

Asked most often in Preston

Do new build houses still need air conditioning?

A lot of them do, and the reason is the glazing. Modern homes are built airtight and well insulated, which is excellent in winter and means solar gain through large south and west facing windows has nowhere to go in summer. A well insulated new build can run hotter than a draughty Victorian house in July.

How far out from Preston do you travel?

Preston is about 45 minutes from our Manchester base, which puts it comfortably inside our normal working area along with Chorley, Leyland and central Lancashire. Further north than Lancaster starts to stretch the callout response, and we would rather say so than promise a same week visit we cannot make.
