Why people fit air conditioning at home

It is not just for heatwaves. A fitted system earns its keep all year.

Sleep properly on hot nights

It holds the bedroom at the temperature you set, all night, quietly. No fan blowing in your face, no window open to the street.

Summers are getting hotter

Summer 2026 was the hottest on record for the UK and for North West England, with 55 days above 28°C. Seven of the North West's ten warmest summers have come in 2003 or later.

Cheaper heating in winter

It moves heat in from outside, giving several units of heat for each unit of electricity. That makes it far cheaper than a plug in heater, and handy in spring and autumn without firing up the boiler.

Work from home in comfort

A home office in a loft or a south facing room is the first to overheat. Cool, quiet air means you can actually work in it on a July afternoon.

Drier, cleaner air

It takes moisture out of the air as it cools, so the room feels fresher, and its filters catch dust before the air comes back into the room.

Windows shut, house secure

Keep the windows closed on hot nights: no traffic noise, no insects, and nothing left open while you sleep.

What people ask before booking

Will it be noisy?

Not inside. The compressor, the noisy part, sits in the outdoor unit. Quiet indoor units start around 19 dB(A) on their lowest setting, about a whisper, and the outdoor unit is placed to stay within the noise limit at your neighbours' windows.

How much will it cost to run?

About 8p an hour to keep a bedroom cool once it is down to temperature, and about 17p an hour while it cools a hot room down, at the October 2026 price cap of 26.32p per kWh.

Do I need planning permission?

Usually not for a house in England. One outdoor unit is normally allowed under permitted development if it meets the noise and position rules. Flats, listed buildings and conservation areas have extra steps, which we check at the survey.

How long does it take to fit?

A single room is usually fitted in one day. Several rooms take longer, and the survey gives you the exact time before you commit.

I live in a flat. Can I still have it?

Usually, yes. The lease decides more often than planning does, and the freeholder's consent is normally needed for the outdoor unit. A balcony is often the easiest position. We put together what the freeholder needs at the survey.

Is the price really fixed?

Yes. The survey produces an itemised written price, valid for 60 days, that does not change once you accept it. The prices on this page are guides for a straightforward job.
