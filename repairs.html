What goes wrong

Six faults that account for most of our callouts

We attend breakdowns across the North West. Two of these you can check yourself in five minutes. We would rather tell you that than charge you to come out and press a button on a remote.

Blowing warm air

Check the mode first

Usually the remote sitting in heating or fan mode. If not, it is a dirty filter, a blocked outdoor coil, or refrigerant that has leaked away. A system low on gas will still blow, just not cold.

Typical fix
Clean, or leak test and repair before regassing

Water dripping inside

Nearly always the drain

The condensate tray blocks with dust and biofilm, fills, and overflows into the room. Occasionally it is a pump that has given up, or a drain run that was never laid to a fall.

Typical fix
Clear and flush the drain, replace the pump if fitted

Rattles, hums and squeals

Bearings and mounts

A squeal is usually a fan bearing. A rattle is often a loose panel or a bracket that has worked loose. A deep hum from outside can be a compressor mount that has perished.

Typical fix
Fan motor, anti vibration mounts, or a tightening job

Tripping the electrics

Stop using it

Water where it should not be, a failing compressor, or a damaged cable. Repeatedly resetting a breaker on an appliance that keeps tripping it is how small faults turn into fires.

Typical fix
Insulation test, then the component at fault

Error code on the display

Tell us the code

Every manufacturer uses its own codes and they narrow the job down enormously. Read it out when you ring and there is a good chance the right part is already on the van.

Typical fix
Depends entirely on the code, so tell us what it says

Ice on the pipework

Turn it off

Ice on the copper or the indoor coil means poor airflow or low refrigerant. Running it like that will kill the compressor, which is the one part you really do not want to replace.

Typical fix
Airflow restored, or leak found and repaired

Common questions

Before you ring us

My unit is blowing warm air. Is it broken?

Check the mode first. It is genuinely the most common callout we get, and it is usually a remote that has been nudged from cooling into heating or fan only. The snowflake symbol is cooling, the sun is heating.

If the mode is right and it is still warm, the likely causes are a blocked filter, a dirty outdoor coil, or low refrigerant from a leak. The first two you can look at yourself. The third needs an F-Gas engineer, because topping up a leaking system without finding the leak is both illegal and pointless.

Water is dripping from the indoor unit

Almost always a blocked condensate drain rather than a leak of anything expensive. The tray fills up, water finds the lowest point and comes out of the front of the unit. Turn it off so you are not making more water, put a towel down, and ring us. It is normally a same visit fix.

How much is a callout?

£95 for the first hour including travel, then £45 an hour after that. Parts are quoted before we fit them. Service plan customers pay no call out charge at all, and Plus and Commercial plans get seen before anyone without a contract.

Do you repair systems you did not install?

Yes, and most of our repair work is exactly that. Daikin, Mitsubishi Electric, Panasonic, Toshiba, Samsung, LG, Fujitsu, Hitachi and Midea are all familiar. If a system is beyond economic repair we will say so and show you the figures rather than replacing parts on something that is going to fail again.

The system is 12 years old. Repair or replace?

Depends on what has failed and what gas it uses. A fan motor or a control board on a ten year old unit is worth fixing. A failed compressor usually is not, because the part and the labour together get close to a new system.

The other factor is the refrigerant. Older systems running R22 cannot legally be topped up at all, and R410A is being phased down, which pushes the price of a regas up every year. A current R32 system will also use roughly a third less electricity, which changes the sums.
