Can you put air conditioning in a garden room or garden office?

Reviewed November 2026 by Zac Hancox, Founder of CoolIn

The short answer

Yes, and in an insulated garden room it works very well. A wall mounted unit goes inside, a small outdoor unit stands beside or behind the building, and the pipe run is usually short. It cools the room in summer and heats it far more cheaply than a panel heater in winter, which makes a garden office usable all year.

  • Garden rooms overheat fast, because of the glass front and a roof in full sun
  • The same unit heats it in winter, far more cheaply than an electric panel heater
  • The outdoor unit usually stands on the ground beside the building, close to the room
  • The power supply from the house has to have room for it, which we check at survey
  • Plan it before the garden room is built if you can, so the power and pipes are ready
  • One room from £1,650 fitted

A garden office is a brilliant place to work for most of the year. Then the first warm week arrives, the sun hits the glass doors, and by lunchtime it's too hot to think. In January it's the opposite, and an electric heater struggles to keep up. Fitted air conditioning deals with both.

Why do garden rooms get so hot?

  • A wall of glass. Most garden rooms have glazed doors or a glass front facing the garden, and often the sun.
  • A small space. There isn't much air to absorb the heat, so the temperature climbs quickly.
  • A roof in full sun all day, usually with less height and insulation above you than a room in the house.
  • Computers and screens add their own heat, all day.

The same small, glazed building loses heat fast in winter, which is why so many garden offices end up with an electric heater running for hours.

What size unit does a garden room need?

Most garden rooms take a 2.0 or 2.5kW wall unit. A larger room, or one with a lot of south or west facing glass, can need 3.5kW. The size calculator has an option for bifolds and large glazing, and gives a starting figure in under a minute.

Where do the units go?

  • Inside: a wall mounted unit high on the back or side wall, away from where you sit, so the air doesn't blow straight at you. It's fixed into the frame of the building.
  • Outside: the outdoor unit usually stands on the ground beside or behind the garden room, on a level base or a small stand, or on a bracket fixed into the frame. Either way the pipe run is short.

Garden rooms often sit close to a fence or a neighbour's garden, so we place the outdoor unit for noise as well as access.

What about the power supply?

Most garden rooms are fed by an armoured cable from the house to a small board inside the garden room. A single wall unit is a modest load, but the cable and the board need room for it. We check both at survey and tell you whether anything needs upgrading.

If your garden room isn't built yet, mention air conditioning to the builder now. A suitable power supply, a sleeve through the wall for the pipes and a spot for the outdoor unit cost very little at the build stage, and save work later.

Will it heat the garden room in winter?

Yes. It works as an air to air heat pump, giving several units of heat for each unit of electricity, so it heats far more cheaply than a panel heater or a fan heater, which only ever give one. For a garden office used every day through the winter, that's where most of the saving is. The article on heating with air conditioning covers the best settings.

Do you need planning permission?

Usually not. The garden room itself is normally an outbuilding under permitted development, and an outdoor unit beside it is normally covered by the same rules as one on the house, including the noise limit of 42 dB(A) at the nearest neighbour's window. Conservation areas and listed buildings add restrictions. The planning permission article sets out the rules, and we check the position at survey.

Does it work in a shed or summer house?

Only if it's insulated. In an uninsulated timber building, the heat pours back in through the walls and roof as fast as the unit takes it out. Insulate it first, and a small unit will keep it comfortable.

Questions people ask next

Will air conditioning work in an uninsulated shed or summer house?

Not well. Without insulation the heat pours straight back in through the walls and roof, so the unit runs flat out and the room never gets comfortable. Insulate it first, and then a small unit works well.

Do I need planning permission for air conditioning on a garden room?

Usually not. An outdoor unit in the garden or on the garden room is normally covered by the same permitted development rules as one on the house, including the noise limit at the nearest neighbour's window. Garden rooms often sit near a boundary, so the position matters, and we check it at survey.

Can the air conditioning run from my garden room's existing power supply?

Often, yes. Most garden rooms are fed by an armoured cable from the house. A single wall unit is a modest load, but the cable and the board in the garden room need the capacity, and we check that at survey.

Is a garden office air conditioning installation zero rated for VAT?

Home installations are zero rated until 31 March 2027, but a garden building used purely as a business office may not count as part of the home. We confirm which applies at survey and show it in the written price.

How long does it take to fit?

Usually one day. The pipe run from the outdoor unit is short, and the walls of a timber garden room are quick to drill and seal.
