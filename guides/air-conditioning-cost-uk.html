How much does air conditioning cost in the UK?

Reviewed November 2026 by Zac Hancox, Founder of CoolIn

The short answer

Fitted air conditioning for one room typically starts from around £1,650 installed for a 2.5kW wall unit, and from around £3,450 for two rooms on one outdoor unit. Home installations carry no VAT until 31 March 2027. Running a bedroom unit costs roughly 7p to 16p an hour.

  • One room from £1,650 fitted, for a 2.5kW wall mounted unit
  • Two rooms on one outdoor unit from £3,450, five rooms from £8,200
  • No VAT on home installations until 31 March 2027, then 5%
  • A single room is usually fitted in one day
  • Running a bedroom unit costs roughly 7p to 16p an hour
  • Servicing from £89 per indoor unit per year

The prices below are CoolIn's installed prices, not averages scraped from around the internet. Each one includes the equipment, the fitting, the electrical connection and commissioning. That makes them a fair yardstick for any quote you get, wherever you are in the UK.

How much does a fitted air conditioning unit cost for one room?

A wall mounted split, with one indoor unit on the wall and one outdoor unit outside, is the most common installation in UK homes. The price depends mainly on the unit size, and the unit size depends on the room.

A bigger unit isn't a safer choice. An oversized unit switches on and off more often, controls humidity less well and costs more to buy. The size calculator gives a starting figure for any room in under a minute.

How much does it cost to air condition a whole house?

A multi split runs two to five indoor units from a single outdoor unit, with a separate controller in each room.

RoomsInstalled price
Two rooms£3,450
Three rooms£4,995
Four rooms£6,650
Five rooms£8,200

Each extra room costs less than the first, because the rooms share one outdoor unit and one set of electrics. On a house that's not detached, a multi split is often the only way to cool several rooms without planning permission, since permitted development allows one outdoor unit.

If you'd rather see nothing on the walls at all, a ducted system hides the indoor unit in the loft or ceiling and costs from around £3,500 for two rooms.

What are you paying for?

The unit itself is only part of the price. A fitted installation covers:

  • Indoor and outdoor units, matched as a pair
  • Wall brackets or floor feet, and anti vibration mounts
  • Up to five metres of insulated pipe in slim white trunking
  • One neat hole through the wall, sealed inside and out, and a condensate drain
  • The electrical connection, an isolator and testing
  • Pressure and vacuum testing, then commissioning
  • The manufacturer's warranty registered in your name

Two engineers usually fit a single room in a day. The refrigerant work must be done by an F-Gas certified technician, which is one reason a fitted system costs more than the box it comes in.

Why do quotes for the same job vary so much?

Because they often aren't for the same job. Before comparing two quotes, check each one includes:

  • The same unit size, brand and model range
  • The length of pipe run included, and the price per extra metre
  • The electrical connection, or whether you need your own electrician
  • Commissioning and warranty registration
  • Making good the wall and removing packaging
  • The VAT position, written down

A quote that's £300 cheaper but leaves out the electrics or the pipe run usually ends up costing more. Very low prices sometimes mean the installer isn't registering the warranty or isn't handling the refrigerant properly.

What makes air conditioning cost more?

  • Pipe runs longer than five metres, or routes through more than one floor
  • An outdoor unit on a flat roof, a high bracket or a lightwell
  • Scaffolding or a tower for access
  • Chasing pipework into the walls instead of running it in trunking
  • A new circuit from the consumer unit
  • Designer finishes or premium model ranges
  • Listed buildings, conservation areas and flats needing the freeholder's consent

How the home is built matters as much as its size. The guides for a terraced house, a flat or apartment, a loft conversion, a conservatory and a garden room each explain what changes the price for that type of space.

Is a portable air conditioner cheaper?

To buy, yes, by a long way. To run, no. A typical portable draws about 1kW, roughly three times what a fitted unit uses to hold a bedroom cool, and it's much louder in the room. A portable suits renters and the odd hot week. If you'd use it every summer, fitted air conditioning usually works out cheaper over a few years. The guide to portable or fitted air conditioning compares the two in detail.

Does air conditioning carry VAT in the UK?

Not on a home installation until 31 March 2027. Energy saving materials fitted in homes are zero rated, and HMRC treats air conditioning units that heat as well as cool as air source heat pumps. The relief only applies when the same company supplies and installs the system, so a unit you buy yourself carries 20% VAT.

From 1 April 2027 the rate is set to return to 5%. On a £3,450 two room system, that adds about £170. Commercial installations carry VAT at the standard rate, which a VAT registered business can normally reclaim.

How much does air conditioning cost to run?

Less than most people expect. At an electricity price of 25p per unit, a 2.5kW bedroom unit costs:

Running statePower drawnCost per hour
Holding the temperatureabout 0.3 kWabout 7p
Working hard on a hot afternoonabout 0.65 kWabout 16p

Most units also heat. Because they move heat rather than make it, a current unit delivers around four and a half units of heat for each unit of electricity, which is why heating a room with air conditioning can cost less than a plug in heater or a gas boiler. How much does commercial air conditioning cost?

Commercial prices are shown plus VAT. A single 5kW wall unit in a small shop or office starts at £2,280, a four cassette system for an office floor is around £8,500, and a six unit VRF system around £14,000. The commercial page breaks prices down by type of building.

Questions people ask next

Is air conditioning worth it in the UK?

For most homes that use it every summer, yes, because a fitted system also heats in winter, often more cheaply than a gas boiler or a plug in heater. It makes the most difference in bedrooms that overheat at night, loft conversions, conservatories and south facing rooms.

Can I install air conditioning myself?

Not a fitted split system. Connecting the pipework and handling the refrigerant must be done by an F-Gas certified technician, and the 0% VAT relief only applies when the same company supplies and installs the system. A self installed unit also usually loses the manufacturer's extended warranty.

How long does it take to fit air conditioning?

A single room is usually one day. A multi split serving two to five rooms is usually two days. Ducted systems and jobs that need scaffolding or a new electrical circuit can take longer, which the survey confirms before work starts.

Is it cheaper to air condition one room or the whole house?

One room costs less in total, but each extra room costs less than the first. A multi split shares one outdoor unit between rooms, so two rooms from £3,450 works out cheaper than two separate single systems at £1,650 each, and you only have one unit outside.

Do I need planning permission for air conditioning?

Usually not for a house in England, where one outdoor unit is normally allowed under permitted development if it meets the noise and position rules. Flats, listed buildings and conservation areas have extra steps, and a second outdoor unit on a house that's not detached needs permission.

Does air conditioning cost more in some parts of the UK?

Prices vary between installers more than between regions. Labour rates are usually higher in London and the South East, and access, parking and pipe routes in dense city housing can add time. The things that move the price most are the same everywhere, mainly the pipe run, access and where the outdoor unit can go.
