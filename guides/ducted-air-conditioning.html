What is ducted air conditioning, and is it worth it for a home?

Reviewed November 2026 by Zac Hancox, Founder of CoolIn

The short answer

Ducted air conditioning hides the indoor unit in a loft, ceiling void or bulkhead and sends cool or warm air to each room through ducts, so all you see is a slim grille. Add zoning and every room sets its own temperature. It costs more than wall units and suits a renovation or new build best, from £3,500 fitted for two rooms.

  • Nothing on the walls, just a slim grille in the ceiling of each room
  • One hidden unit can serve two or three rooms, and larger homes use more than one
  • Zoning gives each room its own thermostat, using motorised dampers in the ducts
  • It needs a loft, ceiling void or bulkhead deep enough for the unit
  • Easiest during building work; retrofitting into finished ceilings is more disruptive
  • From £3,500 fitted for two rooms, with no VAT to add until 31 March 2027

Ducted air conditioning is the option for people who want cooling and heating in every room without a single unit on the walls. It's the hardest kind to retrofit and the easiest to live with, which is why it's most popular in new builds, big renovations and period homes where a wall unit would look wrong.

How does ducted air conditioning work?

The indoor unit sits out of sight, usually in the loft, a ceiling void or a boxed in bulkhead. It draws air from the house through a return grille, cools or heats it, and pushes it through insulated ducts to a slim grille in the ceiling of each room. The outdoor unit sits outside, as with any fitted system, joined to the indoor unit by two copper pipes.

All you see in each room is a narrow slot or a square grille, plus a controller on the wall.

What is zoning?

Zoning lets each room set its own temperature from one ducted unit. Motorised dampers in the ducts open and close to send more or less air to each room, and each room gets its own thermostat. A bedroom can sit at 19°C while the study runs at 22°C.

Without zoning, every room on the same unit shares one setting, which is fine for open plan spaces but frustrating for bedrooms. For a whole house, zoning is usually worth it.

What does a ducted system need?

  • Somewhere to hide the unit. A loft above the rooms is easiest. A ceiling void or a bulkhead works too, if it's deep enough for the unit, the ducts and the drain.
  • A route for the ducts to each room, through the loft or between joists.
  • A drain for the condensate, run to the outside or into a waste pipe, with a small pump where gravity won't work.
  • Access for servicing. The filters need cleaning, usually from behind the return grille, and the unit needs an access hatch.

We check all four at the survey and show you where the grilles would go.

Is it worth it?

It's worth it if:

  • you're renovating, extending or building, so the ceilings are open anyway
  • you want nothing on the walls, for looks or because the house is a period property
  • you want to cool or heat several rooms evenly, with zoning
  • you'd like to add fresh air later, through a heat recovery system

It's usually not the best choice if the rooms are finished and you don't want ceilings opening up, or if you only need one or two rooms done. Wall units are cheaper, quicker and just as efficient there.

Does ducted air conditioning heat the house too?

Yes. Like any fitted system, it works as an air to air heat pump, giving several units of heat for each unit of electricity. Because the grilles are in the ceiling and warm air rises, it heats best with the airflow set low and the fan on a steady speed. The guide to heating with air conditioning covers the settings.

What does ducted air conditioning cost?

A ducted system serving two rooms starts at £3,500 fitted, with no VAT to add on home installations until 31 March 2027. Whole house systems, zoning and fresh air are priced at the survey, because the layout of the loft and the number of rooms make the biggest difference. The price guide has every system side by side.

Ducted systems fit naturally into a loft conversion, where the unit can hide in the eaves, and into the large period homes of Altrincham, Wilmslow and south Manchester, where nothing should show on the walls.

Questions people ask next

Can each room have its own temperature with ducted air conditioning?

Yes, with a zoning system. Motorised dampers in the ducts open and close to send more or less air to each room, and each room gets its own thermostat. Without zoning, every room the unit serves shares one setting.

How much space does a ducted unit need?

Slim ducted units are designed for ceiling voids and are only a few hundred millimetres deep, but they also need room for the ducts, the drain and access for servicing. A loft above the rooms is the easiest place. We measure the void at the survey.

Is ducted air conditioning noisy?

It's usually quieter in the room than a wall unit, because the fan sits away from the room in the void and the air comes in through a grille. The ductwork has to be sized properly so the air doesn't whistle at the grilles.

Can ducted air conditioning heat my home?

Yes. It works as an air to air heat pump, the same as a wall unit. Ceiling grilles put warm air in high up, so it heats best with the airflow set low and the fan on a steady speed.

Can ducted air conditioning bring in fresh air?

It can be paired with a fresh air supply, such as a heat recovery ventilation system, which wall units can't do. That suits new builds and airtight homes where stale air is part of the problem.
