If you own a portable air conditioner, the story is often the same. It helped on the first hot night, then the room crept back up, the noise kept you awake, and the window wouldn't close properly. This guide sets out what each kind actually does, so you can decide whether the portable is good enough or it's time for something fitted.

## What is the difference between a portable and fitted air conditioner?

**A portable air conditioner** is one box on wheels that stands in the room, with a wide hose that goes out through a gap in the window. Everything happens inside that box, including the noisy part, the compressor.

**Fitted air conditioning** puts a slim unit high on the wall inside and a small unit outside, joined by two thin copper pipes through one small hole in the wall. The noisy part sits outside. Installers call this a split system, because it's split between an indoor unit and an outdoor one, and it's the kind you see on the wall in hotels and offices.

## Why isn't my portable air conditioner cooling the room?

A portable air conditioner often fails to cool a room because of how a single hose unit works: it blows air out of the window, and warm air is drawn back in to replace it. It takes in air from the room, uses some of it to cool the hot parts inside the unit, and blows that warm air out of the window. Air that leaves the room has to be replaced, so warm air from outside and from the rest of the house is drawn back in through gaps around doors and windows. The unit is cooling the room and warming it at the same time.

The hose itself gets hot and gives off heat, and the gap where it goes through the window lets more warm air in. That's why a portable rated to cool a large room often struggles with a small bedroom on a hot afternoon.

Before you give up on it, check these:

- **The window gap is sealed.** Use the panel or fabric seal that came with it, so the only opening is the hose.
- **The hose is short and straight.** A long or kinked hose holds heat and slows the air.
- **The door is closed.** Otherwise it's trying to cool the landing as well.
- **The filter is clean.** Most need rinsing every couple of weeks in summer.
- **The sun is kept off the glass.** Blinds or curtains closed on the sunny side make a real difference.
- **It's big enough.** The [size calculator](/air-conditioning-size-calculator) gives a fair figure for the room.

If all of that is right and the room is still warm in the afternoon, the portable isn't the wrong size. It's the wrong tool for that room.

## How much does a portable cost to run compared with fitted air con?

A portable air conditioner costs about 26p an hour to run, against about 8p to 17p an hour for fitted air conditioning, at the October 2026 price cap of 26.32p per kWh. A portable draws about 1kW the whole time its compressor runs. A fitted 2.5kW unit draws about 0.65kW while it brings a hot room down, then throttles back to around 0.3kW to hold it there, because it can slow down instead of switching on and off.

## Is fitted air conditioning noisy?

Fitted air conditioning is much quieter than a portable unit, because its compressor, the noisy part, sits outside. Quiet fitted indoor units start around 19 dB(A) on their lowest setting, about the level of a whisper. A portable has its compressor in the room with you, and most run at around 50 to 65 dB(A), closer to the level of normal conversation. For a bedroom, that's often what decides it.

## Can a portable air conditioner heat a room?

Some portable air conditioners can heat a room and many cannot, so check the label. Fitted air conditioning heats as standard, and it does it efficiently: it moves heat in from outside, giving several units of heat for each unit of electricity it uses. That makes it far cheaper to run than any plug in heater, which is why a fitted unit earns its keep in winter as well as summer. The [comparison with a gas boiler](/blog/air-conditioning-vs-gas-boiler-heating) goes through the numbers.

## Is fitted air conditioning worth the extra cost?

Fitted air conditioning isn't worth it on the electricity saving alone: over a 300 hour summer it saves about £30 to £55 against a portable. Fitted saves roughly 9p to 18p an hour against a portable, and 300 hours of that comes to about £30 to £55 a year. That won't pay back the difference in price on its own.

What makes fitted worth it is everything else:

- **It actually cools the room**, on the hottest afternoons, not just the area in front of the unit.
- **You can sleep with it on**, because the noisy part is outside.
- **The window stays shut**, which matters for security, insects and noise from outside.
- **It heats cheaply in winter**, so it replaces a plug in heater and works all year.
- **It takes up no floor space** and there's no hose to trip over or store.

## When is a portable the better choice?

A portable air conditioner is the better choice when:

- **You rent** and can't get permission for anything fixed to the building.
- **You only need it for a few hot days a year**, and don't mind the noise for those.
- **You're moving soon** and want something that comes with you.
- **Your building won't allow an outdoor unit.** Before settling for a portable, ask about a through the wall unit with no outdoor part, which is usually quieter than a portable. The [flats and apartments guide](/guides/air-conditioning-flat-or-apartment) covers both.
