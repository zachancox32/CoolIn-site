Can you put air conditioning in a conservatory?

Reviewed November 2026 by Zac Hancox, Founder of CoolIn

The short answer

Yes, and it's one of the best ways to make a conservatory usable all year. A wall unit on the house wall or a low console on the dwarf wall cools it in summer and heats it in winter. Conservatories need a bigger unit than the floor area suggests, because of all the glass, and blinds help it work far better.

  • Glass and polycarbonate let in heat in summer and lose it fast in winter
  • Usually needs a bigger unit than a room of the same size, often 3.5kW or more
  • The indoor unit goes on the old house wall, or as a low console on the dwarf wall
  • It heats in winter too, far more cheaply than a plug in heater
  • Roof blinds or solar film cut the heat coming in and make the unit work less
  • From £1,650 fitted, and most conservatories need 3.5kW at £1,850, with no VAT to add until 31 March 2027

Most conservatories are lovely for a few weeks in spring and autumn and unusable the rest of the year: an oven in July, a fridge in January. Air conditioning fixes both ends, because the same unit cools in summer and heats in winter.

Why do conservatories get so hot and so cold?

Glass and polycarbonate let the sun's heat straight in, and there's very little to stop it. On a sunny afternoon a conservatory can be far hotter than the garden outside. At night and in winter, the same glass lets the heat straight back out again, so the room goes cold quickly once the sun's gone.

That's why a conservatory needs more cooling and more heating than a room of the same size in the house.

What size air conditioning does a conservatory need?

Usually a size or two up from what the floor area alone suggests. As a rough guide:

  • A small conservatory, out of direct sun: 2.5kW can be enough
  • A small to medium conservatory in the sun: often 3.5kW
  • A large or south facing conservatory, or one with a glass roof: often 5kW

The roof makes the biggest difference. A glass or polycarbonate roof gains far more heat than a solid one. The size calculator has a conservatory option and gives a starting figure, and the survey confirms it.

Where does the indoor unit go?

Conservatories have very little solid wall, so there are two usual places:

  • On the house wall. The wall the conservatory was built against is usually the only full height solid wall, and a wall mounted unit sits high on it, blowing out across the room.
  • A low level floor console on the dwarf wall. If the conservatory has a low brick wall under the windows, a console sits on it, much like a radiator. It's especially good at heating, because it puts warm air in at floor level.

Where does the outdoor unit go?

Usually on the house wall beside the conservatory or on the ground nearby, so the pipe run is short. It has to meet the same rules as any outdoor unit: the noise limit at the neighbour's window, and not on a wall above ground floor level that faces a road. The planning permission article sets out every condition.

What makes air conditioning work better in a conservatory?

  • Roof blinds or solar film. Cutting the sun before it gets in is the cheapest improvement you can make. The unit works less and the room stays more even.
  • Closing the doors to the house, so you're only cooling or heating the conservatory.
  • Leaving it running at a steady temperature rather than switching it on when the room's already hot.

Some people replace the conservatory roof with a solid, insulated one. It makes a big difference to how stable the room feels, and it can mean a smaller unit, but on its own it won't make the room cool on a hot day.

Will it heat the conservatory in winter?

Yes. It works as an air to air heat pump, giving several units of heat for each unit of electricity, so it heats far more cheaply than an electric heater. A conservatory still loses heat faster than a room in the house, so it will cost more to keep warm than a bedroom, but it turns it into a room you can use in winter. The article on heating with air conditioning covers the settings.

Is a portable air conditioner enough?

Rarely. A sunny conservatory gains more heat than a portable unit can remove, and its hose has to go out through an open window or door, which lets warm air back in. The guide to portable or fitted air conditioning explains why.

Questions people ask next

What size air conditioner does a conservatory need?

Usually bigger than a room of the same floor area, because of the glass. A small to medium conservatory often needs 3.5kW, and a large or south facing one 5kW. The size calculator gives a starting figure and the survey confirms it.

Can air conditioning heat a conservatory in winter?

Yes. It works as an air to air heat pump, so it heats far more cheaply than a plug in heater. A conservatory still loses heat quickly through the glass, so keep the doors to the house closed and expect it to cost more to heat than a room in the house.

Is a portable air conditioner enough for a conservatory?

Rarely. A conservatory in summer sun gains more heat than a portable unit can remove, and the hose has to go through an open window or door, letting warm air back in. A fitted system cools it properly and heats it in winter too.

Should I replace the conservatory roof instead?

A solid, insulated roof cuts the heat coming in and makes the room much more stable, and it can be worth doing if the roof needs replacing anyway. It won't make the room cool on a hot day by itself, and many people still add air conditioning afterwards, often a smaller unit.

Do I need planning permission?

Usually not. The outdoor unit is normally covered by permitted development, as long as it meets the size, position and noise rules. Conservation areas and listed buildings add restrictions, and we check the position at survey.
