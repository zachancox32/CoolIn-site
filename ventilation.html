Ventilation in short

Ventilation and air conditioning solve different problems. Air conditioning changes the temperature of air already inside the building. Ventilation replaces that air with fresh air from outside. A room can be perfectly cool and still feel stale, because carbon dioxide, humidity and smells build up regardless of temperature.

Common questions

Ventilation, answered

Does air conditioning bring in fresh air?

No, and this catches people out constantly. A standard split system recirculates the air already in the room, cooling it and filtering out dust as it passes. It removes no carbon dioxide, no cooking smells and no moisture from the building. If a room feels stuffy rather than warm, cooling it harder will not fix it. You need ventilation.

What is MVHR and is it worth it?

Mechanical ventilation with heat recovery. Stale air is extracted from the building and fresh air is drawn in, and the two streams pass either side of a heat exchanger so the outgoing air warms the incoming air without the two ever mixing. Recovery rates of 85% to 90% are normal.

It is worth it in an airtight building, where opening a window is the only alternative and throws away all the heat you have paid for. In a draughty Victorian terrace with gaps everywhere, extract fans usually make more sense for the money.

My commercial kitchen extract is too weak. Can you fix it?

Usually, and the cause is often not the fan. A canopy pulling air out of a sealed kitchen has to get replacement air from somewhere, and if there is no make up air supply the fan is fighting a vacuum. Doors get hard to open, the extract rate collapses and the room fills with heat.

The fix is a make up air system supplying tempered fresh air at the same rate the canopy removes it. We survey the canopy, measure the actual extract rate and size the supply to match.

Can ventilation help with damp and mould?

Often, because most domestic damp is condensation rather than water coming in. Moisture from cooking, showering and drying washing has to leave the building, and in a house with sealed windows and no working extract it lands on the coldest surface it can find. Continuous low rate extract in the wet rooms deals with the cause rather than wiping the symptom off the wall.

What filtration do you fit for allergies?

Indoor units on a home system take a washable pre filter as standard, which catches dust and hair but not pollen. Most manufacturers offer finer filter cassettes that fit the same units, and for treatment rooms or anywhere with a genuine clinical need we specify separate HEPA filtration rather than relying on what came in the box. Whatever is fitted needs cleaning on schedule or it becomes a restriction rather than a filter.
