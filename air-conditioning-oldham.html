Local knowledge

What the buildings in Oldham tend to need

Oldham sits between 150 and 300 metres above sea level in places. Winters run colder and longer than central Manchester, so we size and explain the heating performance carefully here.

Stone terraces and cottages

Thick gritstone walls hold temperature well once they reach it, which cuts both ways: slow to warm and slow to overheat. Core drilling takes considerably longer than through brick and we allow for that rather than discovering it on the day.

Pennine winters

At 250 metres you see more days below 5°C than in the city, and that is where an air source heat pump works hardest for the least return. We are straight about it: the system will still heat, it will just take longer on the coldest mornings and you will want a second heat source.

Mill conversions

Huge volumes, single glazed metal windows in some cases, and exposed brick with no insulation. Spot cooling in the occupied zones beats trying to condition the whole floor plate.

Saddleworth villages

Conservation areas across Uppermill, Delph and Dobcross, plus a fair number of listed cottages. Outdoor unit position is a planning question before it is a technical one.

Local questions

Asked most often in Oldham

Does a heat pump still work up on the Pennine edge in winter?

Yes, but with less output as the temperature drops. Manufacturers rate units down to around minus 15°C and they keep producing heat there, just more slowly and less efficiently than at 7°C. Above about 200 metres you get more days below 5°C than central Manchester, so we size for that and tell you plainly to keep your existing heating for the coldest stretch rather than pretending it will replace it.

Is drilling through stone a problem?

Not a problem, just slower and more expensive in time than brick. A gritstone wall can be 500mm thick and takes a proper wet core drill and patience. It is worth knowing because a quote that assumes a standard 250mm brick wall will not survive contact with a Saddleworth cottage, and that is where mid job price rises come from.
