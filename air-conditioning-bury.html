Local knowledge

What the buildings in Bury tend to need

Bury sits on the boundary between the brick built suburbs to the south and the stone built moorland towns to the north, and you can see it in the jobs.

Victorian and Edwardian semis

High ceilings, bay windows and, almost always, a loft conversion. The converted loft is the room that drives the enquiry, because it takes sun on the roof all day and sits above the warmest air in the house.

Loft conversions

We size rooms in the roof about 20% above what the floor area alone suggests. It is the single most common reason a system fitted by someone else does not cope in August.

Ramsbottom stone property

Thicker walls, longer core drilling and a colder position than the valley floor. Closer to an Oldham job than a Prestwich one.

Market and town centre food

Small kitchens behind small frontages, where extract and make up air have to be designed together in a very tight space.

Local questions

Asked most often in Bury

Why does my loft conversion need more cooling than the room below?

It gains heat from three directions instead of one. The roof above it is in direct sun for most of the day, the structure is lighter than a room lower down so it has less thermal mass to absorb that heat, and warm air from the rest of the house rises into it. We size loft rooms roughly 20% above a plain floor area calculation, which is the difference between holding temperature on a hot day and not.

Can one outdoor unit serve the loft and two bedrooms?

Yes, that is exactly what a multi split is for, and it keeps one condenser on the wall instead of three. The thing to watch is that the outdoor unit caps the total output shared between the indoor units, so we size it for the case where all three rooms are calling at once on a hot evening, not for the average.
